# Cricket Laws and Rules

This document is part of the DocuQA Cricket corpus. It defines the rules used by the corpus for common delivery and dismissal questions.

## Legal deliveries

A legal delivery is a ball that conforms to the laws of cricket. An over consists of six legal deliveries. A delivery that is a no-ball or a wide is not counted as one of the six legal deliveries in the over, so the bowler must deliver another ball.

## No-ball

A no-ball is an illegal delivery. It is called when the bowler breaks a delivery restriction, such as overstepping the popping crease. A no-ball gives the batting side one no-ball extra and the delivery does not count in the over. A batter may still score runs from the bat or by running on a no-ball.

## Wide

A wide is a delivery that passes too far from the batter to be hit with a normal cricket stroke. The umpire calls a wide and awards one wide extra to the batting side. A wide does not count as one of the six legal deliveries, so another delivery is bowled.

## Dismissal and appeals

A batter can be dismissed in several ways, including bowled, caught, leg before wicket, run out, stumped, hit wicket, and obstructing the field. The fielding side normally appeals to the umpire before a dismissal is given.
